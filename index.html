From Gridlock to Insight: A Visual Approach to the Urban Traffic Crisis

Carolyn Lee, Lasya Muthyam, Daniel Wang, Gavin Crisologo, Nathan James

Introduction

Traffic congestion is a significant problem that is associated with longer travel times, a higher number of traffic accidents, and increased air pollution. These impacts can lead to further downstream issues, including higher costs of goods from increasing shipping times, people missing important travel connections or medical appointments, and higher rates of asthma and respiratory disease.

However, designing better city network hierarchies and street layouts has the potential to alleviate urban congestion by improving traffic flow. We would like to illustrate how different street patterns influence traffic density by creating an interactive visualization, allowing the viewer to better understand how city design can improve traffic congestion and density.

The dataset we would like to use is the TomTom Traffic Stats dataset. This dataset provides detailed statistics on travel times, travel speeds, route accessibility, and congestion bottlenecks using anonymized data gathered from millions of navigation devices. Because the dataset provides granular records of traffic density patterns with trillions of data points over multiple decades of time, it is a significant source of data that will enable us to provide a thorough and detailed analysis on how street patterns influence traffic density in different geographical regions and times of day.
